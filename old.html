Hakone vs Kawaguchiko

Context for the Day 12–13 decision above — both sit near Mt. Fuji but offer very different days.

Aspect Hakone Kawaguchiko
LocationKanagawa, southeast of FujiYamanashi, north side of Fuji
Main drawVolcanic valley (Owakudani), lake cruise, onsen resort townTwo iconic Fuji + torii/pagoda photo spots, lakeside town
Signature experienceRopeway over active sulfur vents, black eggs, Lake Ashi pirate-ship cruiseChureito Pagoda (398 steps) + Tenku no Torii hike
Fuji visibilityOnly from certain spots — often partial or hazyThe entire point of the visit — both shrines are built to frame Fuji
FeelA resort loop — varied attractions all dayTwo intense photo-hikes, otherwise a quiet lake town
Best time of dayAny time; ropeway runs 9am–5pmSunrise/early morning specifically — Fuji clouds over by late morning
Transport from OsakaShinkansen direct to Odawara, ~2.5–3 hrsShinkansen to Mishima/Shin-Fuji + bus transfer, ~3.5–4 hrs
Onsen/ryokanExtensive, some of Japan's best-known ryokanAlso plentiful, many with lake + Fuji views
Effort requiredMostly ropeway/cable car — low physical effort398 steps + a 20–30 min uphill hike, back to back
CrowdsHeavy, especially weekendsChureito Pagoda busy at sunrise; Tenku no Torii much quieter
CostHakone Free Pass ~¥6,100 covers train/ropeway/cable car/boat/busMostly bus fares (~¥300–2,000) + small shrine donations (¥100–200)
The trade-off: Hakone is a fuller, more varied day even if Fuji doesn't show — you still get the valley, the cruise, the onsen. Kawaguchiko is higher-risk, higher-reward: a clear dawn gets you the exact postcard shot, but a cloudy one means two strenuous hikes for very little, since neither spot has much else to offer beyond the Fuji view itself.
